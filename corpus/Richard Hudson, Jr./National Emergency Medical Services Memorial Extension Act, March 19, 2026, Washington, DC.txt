National Emergency Medical Services Memorial Extension Act


https://justfacts.votesmart.org/public-statement/1700955/national-emergency-medical-services-memorial-extension-act


Mr. HUDSON. Madam Speaker, I rise today in support of H.R. 2196, to  extend and authorize the commemorative work to complete the National  Emergency Medical Services Memorial, and urge the House to pass this  bill.

The authorization for work on the memorial expired in November of  2025, and at no cost to the taxpayer, this bill simply allows the  Foundation to continue their donations and work on the memorial in  Washington.

Each year across our Nation, the 850,000 men and women of EMS answer  more than 30 million calls to serve 22 million patients at a moment's  notice. EMS is one of the first lines of defense in the prevention and  mitigation of risk from all sorts of accidents, attacks, or natural  disasters.

In my home state of North Carolina, our EMS serves a vital purpose  when hurricanes hit, and most recently, Hurricane Helene devastated the  western part of the state. EMS workers were risking their lives to aid  their communities, keep people alive, transport friends and neighbors  to safer locations and secure facilities to be treated.

In fact, statistics show EMS providers die in the line of duty at a  rate more than twice the national average for many other careers and  endure an injury rate of nearly 100 percent. Without the sacrifice of  EMS workers, our communities would be much less safe.

It is time to recognize the efforts of these unsung heroes and  memorialize the ultimate sacrifice made by more than 600 members of the  Nation's EMS and their loved ones.

The original bill was passed in 2018, and I look forward to seeing  the work continue so that we can have a memorial in D.C. where our EMS  providers can be commemorated and honored.
